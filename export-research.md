# Indian rice export markets

Mockup-stage research, saved from the ApnaChawal design conversation on 24 October 2026. This is not a launch plan, not a sourcing brief, and not a claim the shop can print on a bag.

Figures are for the Indian financial year 2024–25 unless noted. Sources are APEDA and the Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics, plus trade reports in The Times of India and The Hindu BusinessLine for basmati destination tonnes and unit prices.

## Biggest markets by volume

India shipped about 20.2 million tonnes of rice, worth 12.5 billion dollars.

- Non-basmati: 14.1 million tonnes, 6.5 billion dollars, about 460 dollars a tonne.
- Basmati: 6.1 million tonnes, 5.9 billion dollars, about 980 dollars a tonne.

Ordinary rice moves in bulk to West Africa. APEDA’s leading non-basmati destinations were Benin (15.7 percent), Guinea (8.2 percent), Côte d’Ivoire (8.0 percent), Togo (6.5 percent), and Bangladesh (5.5 percent).

## Price per tonne

Basmati realisations reported for that year:

- United States: about 1,230 dollars a tonne.
- China: about 1,095 dollars a tonne.
- Saudi Arabia: about 1,025 dollars a tonne.
- Iran: about 881 dollars a tonne. Some Iranian parcels were reported under 700 dollars a tonne in early 2025.
- UAE and Saudi Arabia were nearer 900 to 950 dollars in one March 2025 trade note, below the full-year Saudi average above.

The Gulf buys the most aromatic rice. The United States pays the highest price per tonne.

## Europe, UK, Canada

The UK took about 0.18 million tonnes of basmati.

Brown and husked basmati from India into the EU, September 2024 to August 2025, was led by the Netherlands at about 43,000 tonnes, then Italy at about 17,000 tonnes. That line is not all Indian rice.

Canada appears in trade notes as a premium niche. No official tonne figure was in the same tables.

Europe pays up when the grain is brown, aged, or low-residue. The tonnes are small next to the Gulf.

## Gobindobhog, Kalanamak, and the other small rices

No public export ranking exists for Gobindobhog, Kalanamak, or the other heritage rices in the ApnaChawal ten. They do not move in the West Africa bulk trade.

They would sell as small premium lots into the same diaspora shops that already pay for basmati: the Gulf first by volume, then the US, the UK, and Canada by price.

## What export actually requires

Three practical gates, before any brand story:

1. Phytosanitary certification. APEDA’s export checklist also asks for an importer-exporter code, an APEDA registration, and a registration-cum-allocation certificate before a basmati or non-basmati contract ships.
2. Consistent milling at volume. A heritage lot that mills differently every week cannot fill a container the buyer can reorder.
3. Packaging that survives months in a container. The research thread’s own note was vacuum or nitrogen packing so aromatic and unpolished grain does not go rancid or get insects on a long sea leg.
